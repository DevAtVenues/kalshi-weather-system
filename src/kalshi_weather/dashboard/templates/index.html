GLOSSARY

Lock
A contract where the current running high temperature has already passed the strike threshold — the outcome is almost certain. Example: high is 91°F and the contract pays YES if high ≥ 90°F.
Near Lock
The running high is within 3°F of the strike, so the contract outcome is highly probable but not yet certain.
CALIBRATED badge
The model edge was computed using a station-specific bias correction — historical GFS forecast errors for that city were used to adjust the raw model probability. More reliable than Gaussian.
GAUSSIAN badge
The model edge was computed by fitting a normal distribution to the GFS ensemble, then reading off the probability of clearing the strike. Less precise than CALIBRATED — used when no historical bias data is available.
GFS badge
Edge signal comes from a single GFS deterministic run probability estimate, not a full ensemble calculation.
Edge
The difference between our model's probability and the market's implied probability (the mid-price). Edge must exceed fees + spread + a safety buffer before a trade is worth placing.
Market Mid
The midpoint between the YES bid and YES ask prices. Represents the market's consensus probability that the contract resolves YES.
Model P(YES)
The probability that the contract resolves YES according to our model. Compared against the market mid to identify edge.
YES / NO contracts
Each Kalshi weather contract has two sides. Buying YES pays $1 if the condition is met (e.g., high ≥ 90°F). Buying NO pays $1 if the condition is NOT met. Prices are in cents (0–100¢).
T-type contract (T88, T90, etc.)
A "threshold" contract — YES resolves if the daily high is less than (or greater than) the stated temperature. Example: T88 typically means YES if high < 88°F.
B-type contract (B90.5, B92.5, etc.)
A "between" contract — YES resolves if the daily high falls within a 1°F band. Example: B90.5 means YES if 90°F ≤ high < 91°F. Much harder to hit; often low-probability.
GFS
Global Forecast System — NOAA's primary deterministic weather model, updated every 6 hours. Our main forecast source.
GFS Ensemble (GEFS)
31 slightly perturbed versions of the GFS model run together. The spread across members gives a probability distribution, not just a single forecast.
ECMWF Ensemble (ENS)
51-member ensemble from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts. Often more accurate than GEFS, especially 3-7 days out.
LST (Local Standard Time)
The standard (non-daylight-saving) timezone for a city. Kalshi weather contracts settle on the NWS calendar day in LST — e.g., NYC uses EST (UTC-5) year-round for settlement purposes, not EDT.
NWS CLI / CF6
The official NWS Daily Climate Report. This is the exact temperature figure that Kalshi uses to settle weather contracts — not the app, not a weather website.
Settlement Station
The specific NWS observation station Kalshi uses for each city. NYC settles on KNYC (Central Park), not JFK or LGA. The station can differ from the city's main airport — always check the contract rules.
Running High
The highest temperature recorded at the settlement station so far today. Used to determine if a lock condition has already been met before the day ends.
Spread
The difference between the YES ask and YES bid prices. A wider spread means higher transaction cost. We add half the spread to our hurdle rate when calculating whether edge is sufficient.
Fill Model
Since we can't guarantee a limit order fills, we model it: maker fills only if a trade printed at or through our limit price; taker fills at the worst price in the bar. All results reported under both optimistic and pessimistic scenarios.
Taker Fee
Kalshi charges 0.07 × p × (1−p) per contract for market orders, where p is the YES price (0–1). At 50¢ YES, that's ~1.75¢/contract. Maker fees are ~25% of that.
Kelly Fraction
A formula for sizing bets proportional to edge and odds. We use ≤0.25× Kelly (fractional Kelly) to reduce variance. Never full Kelly — it maximizes long-run growth but risks large short-term drawdowns.
Circuit Breaker
A daily loss limit (currently 7% of bankroll). If cumulative losses on a given day hit this threshold, all trading stops for the rest of that day. Makes a catastrophic 30-40% loss day structurally impossible.
Block Bootstrap
A statistical method for computing confidence intervals when data points are correlated (e.g., a heat wave produces many correlated trading days). More honest than standard confidence intervals for weather data.
Walk-Forward Validation
Testing a strategy by training on historical data, then testing on the next unseen period, then rolling forward. More robust than a single train/test split — simulates how the strategy would actually behave over time.
